## Chat-sized audit runs — low-overhead handoff (2026-11-05)

Use this section when a user asks to continue a visual/photo audit and prior chats have run long or maxed out. This is a **work-size rule**, not a new phase, file, spreadsheet, or publishing gate.

1. **Resume, don't reconstruct.** Read this workflow, the current family route/site, existing Photo Coverage Log or previously supported findings, and the single existing `Photo Needs Queue`. Start with unreviewed evidence. Do not rerun completed inventories or repeatedly reopen the same images just to produce a new chat summary.
2. **Small independent passes.** For newly needed image-by-image reviews, aim for about **20–35 distinct existing photos or 3–5 species per pass**, whichever is the more manageable limit. Scale down for complicated grasses or ambiguous photos. For already-documented gaps, transfer supported queue entries in a compact batch without a new visual pass. Avoid automatically generating contact sheets, downloads, ZIPs, or new spreadsheets. Existing galleries and approved media are the first sources.
3. **Save before switching tasks.** Update matching rows (Species ID + exact camera-view target) in the **existing** Google Sheet and any established family Photo Coverage Log used for audited coverage, preserving existing columns and source IDs. Save genuine reviewed/uncertain statuses, not guessed absences. Do not write new botanical/photo content into the legacy hub. Protect against parallel automation edits by rereading target rows before overwriting.
4. **Stop at a clean boundary.** End each user-requested pass after the bounded review and confirmed saves. Summarize briefly: family/species, distinct photos actually visually inspected, exact new/updated queue targets, uncertain holds, and the next unreviewed target. Distinguish saved queue progress from photos **actually verified live** on the public family website.
5. **Next chat handoff.** A new chat starts with the user's simple instruction “Continue the next Hays Botany photo-audit batch.” The worker discovers the latest existing queue/coverage records rather than requiring the owner to paste a large handoff, reupload photos, or run terminal commands. If there is no reliable completion checkpoint for a given photo, say so; do not claim it was reviewed.
6. **Automations remain separate.** This section does not create or restart scheduled tasks, change their cadence, authorize downloads, waive license or identity checks, or alter public galleries.

## Mandatory November–February / seasonal photo coverage check (owner approved 2026-11-04)

**During every gallery-quality audit and any future search for specific missing plant parts, check practical winter and dormant-season ID views alongside whole-plant habit, foliage, stems, flowers, fruit and anatomy.** Field crews in the Kyle/Hays County region need to identify plants even when they are not flowering. Evaluate roles only when biologically applicable:

- **Warm-season grasses after dormancy:** a whole rooted standing brown/copper clump, intact dry inflorescence, attached seedheads, basal remnants.
- **Winter-active cool-season grasses/sedges:** green basal leaves, overall clump, blades and collar/ligule when visible. *Winter-green does not mean dormant.*
- **Overwintering seedlings and rosettes:** recognizable basal leaves before flowering, including winter annual weeds and native spring-flowering annuals.
- **Deciduous woody plants:** leafless silhouette and branching, bark, winter buds/leaf scars, persistent fruit or berries.
- **Evergreens/cacti/yuccas:** foliage and winter habit if adding a useful nonduplicate seasonal view.
- **Late-fall/post-freeze stages:** remaining flower clusters, mature fruit and seeds, standing stalks, senescence, resprouting bases, freeze events such as frostweed ice ribbons.

**Checklist for marking gaps:** Inspect live family site images (not file names) and any already-approved archived/pending images **first**. Classify each relevant seasonal role as *present adequate*, *present inadequate*, *not visually reviewed/uncertain*, *confirmed absent*, or *not applicable*. Never call an unreviewed role absent or require winter photos for every species. Add an existing-queue row **only for a meaningful, actionable uncovered view**, recording exact seasonal target, camera view/scale, evidence, priority, family repo and later next step; do not create family spreadsheets or begin broad downloads. Existing general approved photos may still be published immediately—seasonal wishes, arrows and full lessons never block them.

Keep exact photo identity, creator, source, license/permission, credits, original hashes, public-source rights restrictions and website verification unchanged. Consult BONAP/NAPA county-level distributions separately for native status. See the [owner-approved shared standing rule](https://github.com/Crking92/Hays-Botany-Public/issues/1) and `Hays_Botany_ID_Master_Curation_Tracker` → `Project Rules` row 45.

## Routine cycle while refining photos

1. Read the assigned family repository card/gallery and its latest public website, plus any existing queue targets. Confirm source, species name, observed role, photo originality/credit and whether the photo actually renders in the public learner.
2. Review actual images, not file names alone. Inventory recognizable **whole-plant habit, foliage, stems, flower, fruit/seed, seasonal variation**. Some species legitimately lack ordinary showy flowers or fruit photos; use biology-specific labels.
3. Add family/genus-specific diagnostic requirements where they help identify the species, including practical look-alikes. Examples: Poaceae ligule/collar, rooted base, panicle, spikelet, glumes/lemma/awn; Cyperaceae triangular vs rounded culm, sheath, achene and perigynium where applicable; Asteraceae involucre/phyllaries, disk and ray florets, pappus/achenes; Fabaceae stipules, leaflet arrangement, inflorescence, pod; Euphorbiaceae cyathium/glands/capsule; Vitaceae mature leaf, tendril/node/adhesive disk, berries; oaks buds/bark/acorns.
4. **Before calling a photo missing**, check live family media and any already-cleared pending gallery images. Use statuses: `Confirmed absent`, `Present but inadequate`, `Present — adequate`, `Not reviewed`, or `Candidate exists — identity/rights pending`. Do not treat missing role metadata as evidence that a structure is absent.
5. Search the EXISTING master `Photo Needs Queue` by **Species ID + Exact photo target**, update a matching row if one exists, append only when target genuinely new. Preserve its original 14 columns and existing rows. `Existing source evidence` should cite the actual inspected family gallery media or source photo IDs; `Requested camera view / scale` must make collection/download actionable.
6. `New download?` must distinguish `No — covered`, `No — source candidate pending verification`, `Not yet — inspect existing gallery`, and `Later — focused download needed`. Request **later focused download** only for confirmed missing or inadequate high-value structures; no automatic mass downloads until the owner requests the next collection batch.
7. Rank priorities by crew field-ID value, not number of photos. Prioritize critical ID cues and seasonality over decorative variety. Include genus-level look-alike contrasts, especially where reproductive parts distinguish species.
8. Improvement of existing published media: correct identities and licenses when evidence warrants, prefer complementary angles over duplicates, and improve first-photo order. Publish only genuinely reviewed safe changes through the assigned family repository, validate and verify the **public website**, not merely the commit.
9. Keep site attribution and photo-specific rights (CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, conditional NC on genuinely noncommercial use, or exact owner-display permission) in GitHub. Do not relabel private-study or all-rights-reserved photos as CC.
10. BONAP/NAPA county distributions guide native/local priority for Hays and the established nearby western/northern target counties. Do not infer Hays nativity from Texas-wide status.
